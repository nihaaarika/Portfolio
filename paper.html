Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring: A Baseline-to-Model Comparison

Abstract

This paper investigates whether a simple baseline rule can be improved by a machine learning model for identifying content that needs refreshing. We compare a hand-written rule (based on staleness and volume) against a Random Forest Classifier on the same data split. The model observed a [X]% improvement in accuracy over the baseline. However, under an honest grouped validation split, performance dropped, indicating that the model generalizes moderately to new clients. We provide a ranked action playbook with clear human-review guardrails, directional rather than definitive recommendations, and a reproducible notebook.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content decays over time. Editors need a decision-support tool to prioritize which pages to refresh first. The goal is not to automate decisions, but to surface the highest-opportunity pages for human review. This paper supports the decision: "Which pages should an editor look at first?"

2. Data

We used the FlyRank ML Internship dataset (Hugging Face). The data includes page-level metrics such as staleness days, CTR, volume, and position. We excluded any future-window data and label-derived inputs. No client names, domains, or private queries are included. Date windows were restricted to the training period.

3. Methodology

Assumptions: Staleness and low CTR are directional indicators of content decay. Features: staleness_days, ctr, position, volume. Label: Binary classification of "needs refresh" based on a combination of high staleness and high volume. Baseline: A hand-written rule (if staleness > 60 OR volume > 500, predict refresh). Validation Design: We used both a naive random 80/20 split and an honest grouped split (grouping by client) to check for data leakage.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

The results are observed and directional. The accuracy drop under grouped validation suggests the model's performance on brand-new clients is moderate. These results are decision-support, not definitive predictions. External events (e.g., viral trends) are not captured. The model does not account for business strategy or legal requirements.

6. Ranked Recommendations (Action Playbook)

Reason Codes: STALE_HIGH_VOLUME (Urgent Refresh), DECAY_RISK (Review), LOW_QUALITY (Rewrite).

Archetype Mapping: Evergreen Guides → Urgent Refresh or Rewrite. News Updates → Archive or Review. Product Pages → Optimize or Keep.

Human Review Rules: Every action must be reviewed by an editor. Never auto-delete or auto-publish. The model does not override business logic.

Monitoring Triggers: Retrain if CTR shifts > 15% in a month or model accuracy drops below 70%.
